# Chapter 8 - Pruning, Staking, Wind and Winter Protection

## 8.1 Pruning objectives

Pruning should remove dead, broken or diseased branches, develop a stable structure, provide clearance, renew selected shrubs and preserve natural form. It should not be used to keep an inherently large tree permanently in a tiny space.

## 8.2 At planting

Remove only dead branches, broken branches, severely damaged or rubbing growth, and a clearly defective competing leader when appropriate. Do not remove a large portion of a healthy crown to compensate for root loss.

## 8.3 Structural pruning of young deciduous trees

During the first several years:

- maintain one dominant leader where appropriate;
- select well-spaced scaffold branches;
- remove or shorten strongly competing leaders;
- avoid clusters of branches from one point;
- correct defects while cuts are small.

Never remove more live crown than necessary in one year.

## 8.4 Conifers

Do not top pines, spruces or larches. Preserve the central leader. Remove dead or broken branches and correct a damaged leader carefully.

## 8.5 Shrub pruning

### Lilac

Remove spent flower clusters only if practical. Renew old shrubs by removing a portion of the oldest stems at ground level. Avoid shearing all growth into a dense outer shell.

### Caragana

Maintain as a natural windbreak shrub or clipped hedge. Renewal pruning can remove old stems. Avoid severe late-season pruning.

### Spiraea

Pruning depends on whether it flowers on old or new wood. Identify the species or cultivar before pruning.

## 8.6 Correct pruning cuts

Locate the branch collar and cut just outside it. Do not leave a long stub and do not cut flush with the trunk. For large branches, use a three-cut method to prevent bark tearing. Do not routinely apply wound paint.

## 8.7 Timing

- dead or hazardous branches: remove when identified;
- structural pruning: often during dormancy;
- spring-flowering shrubs: usually after flowering;
- avoid major pruning during severe cold;
- avoid late-season stimulation of tender growth.

## 8.8 Staking review

Inspect ties after wind events and monthly during the growing season. Correct girdling, rubbing, excessive tightness, broken stakes and trunk leaning. Remove staking when the root system can stabilize the tree.

## 8.9 Wind protection

For vulnerable young evergreens, install a permeable screen on windward and sunny sides, keep material from touching foliage, allow ventilation, and remove or open protection after severe conditions pass. Do not wrap the entire crown in impermeable plastic.

## 8.10 Trunk sunscald protection

Young smooth-barked deciduous trees may be damaged by winter sun and temperature fluctuation, especially on south and southwest sides. Use a light-colored breathable guard, correctly managed seasonal wrap, shade board or strategic shrub shading. Inspect guards so they do not trap moisture or harbor rodents.

## 8.11 Root-zone winter protection

- maintain mulch;
- water before freeze-up if autumn is dry;
- prevent salt-contaminated snow from being piled over roots;
- protect from repeated traffic on frozen soil;
- use guards against rodents and browsing.

## 8.12 Snow and ice

Do not beat frozen branches. Gently remove heavy loose snow by lifting branches from below when safe. Allow ice to melt naturally unless a hazard requires professional action.

## 8.13 Frost heave

Small seedlings may be lifted by freeze-thaw cycles. Maintain mulch, firm loose soil gently, correct drainage and re-seat lifted seedlings during suitable conditions.

## 8.14 Winter inspection checklist

- ties loose and intact;
- guards not constricting;
- root flare visible;
- mulch not touching bark;
- evergreen foliage protected from severe exposure;
- no salt storage near roots;
- branches clear of roof-snow fall zones.
