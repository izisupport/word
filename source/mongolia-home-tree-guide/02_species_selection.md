# Chapter 2 - Selecting Trees and Shrubs

## 2.1 Selection principles

Choose species using five filters:

1. **Winter hardiness and provenance**
2. **Water requirement**
3. **Wind and sun tolerance**
4. **Mature size**
5. **Purpose in the design**

Local or regionally proven planting material is preferred. The STREAM nursery report stresses the importance of provenance and locally appropriate planting stock. A species name alone does not guarantee adaptation; seed origin and nursery conditioning matter.

## 2.2 Recommended functional groups

### Siberian larch - *Larix sibirica*

**Use:** tall specimen, seasonal shade, forest character  
**Strengths:** native regional relevance, cold hardiness, attractive soft needles and autumn color  
**Limitations:** needs good light; newly planted trees require dependable moisture; not suitable for tiny spaces  
**Placement:** open sunny area with adequate rooting volume  
**Notes:** select a compact, fibrous root system. Avoid tall, weak nursery stock with a poor root-to-shoot balance.

### Scots pine - *Pinus sylvestris*

**Use:** evergreen structure, wind-filtering, year-round color  
**Strengths:** cold hardy, light-demanding, relatively drought tolerant after establishment  
**Limitations:** young trees may suffer from dry roots, poor handling and winter desiccation  
**Placement:** sunny, well-drained site  
**Notes:** do not bury the root collar. Maintain mulch without touching the trunk.

### Siberian pine - *Pinus sibirica*

**Use:** premium evergreen specimen  
**Strengths:** ornamental form, ecological value  
**Limitations:** slower growth, greater need for careful establishment, may benefit from early shading in nursery production  
**Placement:** protected, well-watered site with sufficient long-term space

### Siberian spruce - *Picea obovata*

**Use:** evergreen screen, formal specimen, winter structure  
**Strengths:** dense foliage and strong visual form  
**Limitations:** can suffer in hot, dry, reflected-heat positions; shallow roots make moisture management important  
**Placement:** cooler, protected area; avoid the hottest south wall

### Siberian fir - *Abies sibirica*

**Use:** ornamental evergreen in favorable microclimates  
**Strengths:** refined foliage and form  
**Limitations:** less tolerant of exposed, hot, dry sites; requires careful nursery and site selection  
**Placement:** sheltered, cooler position with consistent moisture

### Siberian elm - *Ulmus pumila*

**Use:** shade, screening, hardy urban planting  
**Strengths:** tough, fast-growing and tolerant of continental conditions  
**Limitations:** can seed freely; weak branch structure if never trained; variable ornamental quality  
**Placement:** sunny site with room for crown development  
**Management:** begin structural pruning early.

### Birch - *Betula* species

**Use:** light canopy, white bark, visual contrast  
**Strengths:** attractive bark and fine texture  
**Limitations:** generally less drought tolerant than elm or caragana; susceptible to stress on hot, dry sites  
**Placement:** cooler, irrigated area; avoid compacted soil and severe reflected heat

### Poplar and aspen - *Populus* species

**Use:** rapid screening, large properties, windbreak systems  
**Strengths:** fast growth  
**Limitations:** large size, suckering in some types, brittle branches, high water demand in many situations  
**Placement:** only where mature size and roots can be accommodated  
**Caution:** unsuitable close to buildings, pipes, paving and small yards.

### Willow - *Salix* species

**Use:** wet areas, fast screening, cuttings  
**Strengths:** easy vegetative propagation and rapid growth  
**Limitations:** high water demand, large roots, branch breakage in some forms  
**Placement:** only where moisture and space are adequate; not beside foundations or buried services.

### Sea buckthorn - *Hippophae rhamnoides*

**Use:** edible and wildlife planting, thorny boundary hedge, erosion control  
**Strengths:** cold and drought tolerance, nitrogen-fixing association, bright fruit  
**Limitations:** suckering and thorns; male and female plants are usually required for fruit  
**Placement:** full sun, boundary or productive zone

### Siberian peashrub / caragana - *Caragana arborescens*

**Use:** windbreak, hedge, dust filtering, low-maintenance structure  
**Strengths:** very hardy, drought tolerant after establishment, adaptable  
**Limitations:** can become coarse without pruning; may self-seed  
**Placement:** exposed edges and layered shelterbelts

### Lilac - *Syringa* species and cultivars

**Use:** flowering screen, fragrance, ornamental grouping  
**Strengths:** cold hardy, reliable flowering in sun  
**Limitations:** flowering declines in shade; old stems need renewal pruning  
**Placement:** sunny, moderately fertile, well-drained soil

### Spiraea - *Spiraea* species

**Use:** low flowering mass, front boundary, foundation planting  
**Strengths:** hardy, manageable size, useful seasonal color  
**Limitations:** species and cultivar vary in flowering time and pruning requirement  
**Placement:** sun to light shade

### Cotoneaster - *Cotoneaster* species

**Use:** hedge, low screen, berries, formal or informal structure  
**Strengths:** generally hardy and suitable for clipping  
**Limitations:** disease susceptibility varies; dense hedges require airflow and sanitation  
**Placement:** sun or light shade according to species

### Honeysuckle - *Lonicera* shrubs

**Use:** flowering hedge, wildlife value  
**Strengths:** many hardy shrub forms  
**Limitations:** some non-native species can spread; verify local suitability  
**Placement:** mixed shrub border

### Rowan / mountain ash - *Sorbus* species

**Use:** ornamental berries, small-canopy tree, wildlife value  
**Strengths:** attractive fruit and autumn interest  
**Limitations:** drought and heat stress on exposed sites; disease varies by species  
**Placement:** cooler, irrigated part of the yard

### Crabapple and hardy apple - *Malus* species

**Use:** spring flowers, fruit, small ornamental tree  
**Strengths:** multiple seasons of interest  
**Limitations:** pest and disease pressure; requires pruning and sanitation  
**Placement:** sunny, ventilated position, away from constantly wet soil

### Hardy cherry and plum - *Prunus* species

**Use:** flowering and fruit  
**Strengths:** high ornamental value  
**Limitations:** flower buds may be damaged by late frost; site and cultivar selection are critical  
**Placement:** sunny but not in a frost pocket

### Juniper - *Juniperus* species

**Use:** evergreen groundcover or accent  
**Strengths:** drought tolerance after establishment and year-round form  
**Limitations:** dislikes waterlogged soil; some forms may host rust diseases affecting related plants  
**Placement:** sunny, freely drained area

## 2.3 Species to use cautiously

Use large poplars, willows and other aggressive-rooted trees only on properties with adequate space. Avoid unverified imported ornamentals sold without reliable hardiness or provenance information.

Do not choose a plant only because it survived one winter in a container at a shop. Container roots are more exposed than field roots, and retail survival does not demonstrate long-term landscape adaptation.

## 2.4 Diversity rule

Avoid planting the entire yard with one species. Diversity reduces the risk that a single pest, disease or weather event removes all canopy.

A practical small-yard target is:

- one or two structural tree species;
- two to four shrub species;
- no single species dominating every functional layer.

## 2.5 Purchase checklist

Select plants with:

- a clearly visible root flare or root collar;
- no trunk wounds;
- no severe lean;
- live, flexible twigs;
- normal bud development;
- no circling or girdling roots;
- no sour smell from the root ball;
- a root system proportional to the shoot;
- no signs of prolonged drought;
- provenance and age information where available.

The Mongolia nursery recommendations report that planting success is reduced by oversized stock and poor root systems. For forest planting, they cite an optimal seedling height of approximately 15-20 cm and a root-collar diameter of at least 3 mm, with about three years of cultivation under the described conditions. Ornamental saplings may be larger, but they must have been transplanted or root-managed to retain a compact, fibrous root system.

## 2.6 Decision matrix

| Site condition | Preferred starting group |
|---|---|
| Very exposed and dry | Caragana, hardy elm, Scots pine, sea buckthorn |
| Protected and irrigated | Larch, spruce, lilac, rowan, hardy fruit trees |
| Periodically wet | Suitable willow or poplar, placed far from infrastructure |
| Small urban yard | Lilac, spiraea, cotoneaster, small hardy fruit or rowan |
| Large rural property | Layered shelterbelt with shrubs, deciduous trees and conifers |
| Low-maintenance boundary | Caragana, sea buckthorn, selected hardy shrubs |

Final selection should be made after Chapter 1 site assessment.
