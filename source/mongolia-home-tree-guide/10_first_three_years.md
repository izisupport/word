# Chapter 10 - The First Three Years

## 10.1 Why establishment is a multi-year process

Planting is a single event; establishment is the development of roots into the surrounding soil. In Mongolia's short growing season and dry climate, this commonly requires several years.

## 10.2 Day 0 checklist

- root flare at correct level;
- roots spread or corrected;
- tree vertical;
- soil settled with water;
- mulch installed;
- stake only if needed;
- protection installed;
- planting recorded.

## 10.3 First month

Inspect at least twice per week. Check moisture in the root ball and surrounding soil, settlement, leaning, tie pressure, animal damage, wilt and mulch contact with the trunk. Do not interpret every yellow leaf as fertilizer deficiency.

## 10.4 Months 2-4

- continue moisture-based watering;
- remove weeds before seed formation;
- widen the wetted zone gradually;
- correct stake problems;
- avoid heavy pruning;
- watch for insects and fungal symptoms.

## 10.5 First autumn

- water during dry weather until soil freeze approaches;
- stop nitrogen-rich fertilizer early enough for hardening;
- install rodent and browse protection;
- protect vulnerable trunks from sunscald;
- secure but do not constrict stakes;
- maintain mulch.

## 10.6 First winter

After storms, inspect snow loading, check guards, verify wind screens remain ventilated, avoid salt-contaminated snow around roots and do not walk repeatedly over the root zone.

## 10.7 Second spring

- inspect live buds and twig flexibility;
- remove dead wood after live growth is clear;
- check root flare;
- loosen or remove stakes;
- renew mulch;
- expand irrigation area;
- begin light structural pruning where appropriate.

## 10.8 Second growing season

A healthy tree should show stable foliage color, extension growth appropriate to species, no progressive dieback, improved anchorage and roots extending beyond the original root ball.

Continue deep watering during drought. Do not stop care because new leaves appeared.

## 10.9 Third year

- reduce irrigation frequency but maintain drought support;
- remove remaining staking;
- continue structural pruning;
- enlarge mulch bed;
- inspect for girdling roots;
- reassess whether the selected species fits the site.

## 10.10 Establishment indicators

A tree is approaching establishment when the trunk is stable without staking, soil moisture is being accessed beyond the original root ball, normal seasonal growth occurs, canopy density is stable and drought response is less immediate.

## 10.11 Replacement decision

Replace rather than prolong decline when the trunk is severely damaged, root collar is extensively rotten, major girdling roots cannot be corrected, the species is fundamentally unsuited to the site, repeated dieback leaves poor structure or the plant is overgrown nursery stock with an inadequate root system.
