# Chapter 4 - Planting Stock and Planting Time

## 4.1 Types of planting stock

### Bare-root stock

Roots are exposed and must remain moist and cool.

**Advantages**

- light and economical;
- roots can be inspected;
- often appropriate for small forest-type seedlings.

**Risks**

- roots dry rapidly in Mongolian wind and sun;
- planting must occur promptly;
- damaged or folded roots reduce survival.

### Container-grown stock

Roots and growing medium are held in a container.

**Advantages**

- longer operational planting window;
- root ball is protected during handling;
- useful for ornamental and conifer stock.

**Risks**

- circling roots;
- dry root ball that repels water;
- overgrown plants kept too long in plastic;
- root-bound trees with poor stability.

The STREAM nursery report recommends that ornamental saplings kept in plastic containers should remain there for no more than about six months before planting and must be watered regularly. This reinforces the broader principle: do not leave a growing tree indefinitely in an undersized container.

### Balled-and-burlapped stock

A field-grown root ball is wrapped for transport.

**Advantages**

- larger ornamental material can be moved.

**Risks**

- heavy and difficult to position;
- hidden root collar;
- root loss during digging;
- poor handling can crack the root ball.

Use lifting equipment for heavy stock. Never lift a tree by the trunk.

## 4.2 Quality over height

A shorter, well-rooted plant is usually safer than a tall plant with few fine roots. The Mongolia nursery report attributes mortality to oversized seedlings and poor root systems and emphasizes a favorable root-to-shoot ratio.

For ornamental saplings, ask whether the tree was transplanted or root-pruned during production. The report recommends transplanting at least once, preferably twice, to encourage independent root development and more fine roots.

## 4.3 Best planting periods

### Early spring

Usually the safest general period:

- after soil can be worked;
- before strong shoot growth;
- while temperatures are cool;
- with adequate time for root establishment before summer heat.

Do not plant into a frozen or waterlogged hole.

### Late spring to early summer

Possible for container stock when reliable irrigation is available. Higher temperatures increase water demand. Avoid planting during hot, windy weather.

### Autumn

Can suit hardy deciduous stock after leaf fall but before the ground freezes, especially where soil moisture can be maintained. Risks include insufficient root establishment, winter desiccation, frost heave and animal injury.

Evergreens planted too late may enter winter without adequate root function.

### Mid-summer

Not preferred. Plant only when necessary, using healthy container stock, minimal root disturbance, immediate watering, shading where appropriate and intensive monitoring.

## 4.4 Planting-day weather

Choose cool or overcast conditions, low wind, and moist but workable soil. Avoid severe heat, strong dry wind, frozen soil, saturated soil, and periods when watering cannot be guaranteed.

## 4.5 Handling before planting

The STREAM recommendations emphasize that storage should be as short as possible and roots kept dark, cool and moist. For stored bare-root seedlings, the report describes temperatures above freezing and up to about 4°C with high humidity under nursery storage conditions.

For home planting:

- unload immediately;
- place plants in shade;
- protect roots from wind;
- keep root wrapping moist, not submerged;
- do not leave plants in a closed hot vehicle;
- plant the same day whenever possible.

If bare-root planting is delayed, temporarily heel roots into moist soil in a shaded position.

## 4.6 Root inspection

Reject or correct:

- black, sour-smelling or mushy roots;
- severe circling roots;
- girdling roots around the trunk;
- roots dried to brittleness;
- cracked root balls;
- trunk damage at the root collar.

For container stock, remove the pot and inspect the outer root surface. Loosen fine circling roots. Severe woody girdling roots may make the plant unsuitable.

## 4.7 Planting sequence planning

Before delivery, prepare planting positions, tools, mulch, water, guards, stakes only where needed, labels and records. A plant should not wait in the sun while the hole is dug.
