# Chapter 13 - Sample Yard Plans

These plans are conceptual. Confirm mature dimensions, utilities, soil and local requirements before implementation.

## 13.1 Plan A - Compact urban yard, approximately 400-600 m²

### Objectives

- privacy;
- one shade tree;
- flowering interest;
- low irrigation demand;
- winter structure.

### Suggested composition

- 1 hardy elm or appropriately sized rowan as the main deciduous tree;
- 1 Scots pine in an open sunny corner;
- 3 lilacs as a flowering screen;
- 5 spiraea as a repeated low border;
- 3 junipers in the driest zone;
- broad connected mulch beds.

### Water zones

- Zone 1: newly planted trees, deep watering;
- Zone 2: lilac and spiraea;
- Zone 3: established juniper.

### Avoid

- willow and large poplar;
- tall trees under wires;
- narrow lawn strips around trunks.

## 13.2 Plan B - Family yard, approximately 800-1,200 m²

### Objectives

- wind reduction;
- shade;
- edible fruit;
- children's open area;
- four-season structure.

### Suggested composition

Windward boundary:

- staggered caragana hedge;
- two or three Scots pines set behind the shrub line.

Living area:

- one Siberian larch;
- one hardy crabapple or apple;
- lilac group;
- spiraea foreground.

Productive edge:

- sea buckthorn group with compatible male and female plants;
- hardy currants or other locally adapted shrubs.

Keep the central activity area open and preserve irrigation access.

## 13.3 Plan C - Large rural property, 2,000-5,000 m²

### Objectives

- shelterbelt;
- dust reduction;
- snow management;
- visual enclosure;
- habitat.

### Five-zone concept

1. outer tough shrub row: caragana and sea buckthorn;
2. medium deciduous row: elm or locally suitable deciduous species;
3. tall row: Scots pine and larch;
4. inner ornamental zone: lilac, rowan, fruit trees;
5. protected living garden.

Use staggered rows and maintain replacement access.

## 13.4 Plan D - Cool protected courtyard

### Objectives

- evergreen character;
- shade tolerance;
- calm atmosphere.

Possible palette:

- one spruce or fir in the coolest adequately moist position;
- birch or rowan where irrigation is available;
- lilac at the sunny edge;
- low spiraea;
- organic mulch;
- seating outside the future root flare area.

Avoid reflected heat and roof-dry zones.

## 13.5 Plan E - Very dry exposed yard

### Objectives

- survival first;
- progressive shelter;
- minimum permanent irrigation.

Phase 1:

- establish caragana and selected sea buckthorn;
- install temporary wind-permeable fencing;
- create mulch basins;
- improve water storage.

Phase 2:

- add Scots pine and hardy elm in protected positions.

Phase 3:

- add ornamental lilac or spiraea after the microclimate improves.

## 13.6 Plant-count rule

Plant only the number that can be watered through at least the first two to three growing seasons. A phased plan is more reliable than one large planting.

## 13.8 Budget priorities

Spend first on:

1. healthy locally appropriate stock;
2. water delivery;
3. mulch;
4. animal protection;
5. correct planting labor.

Decorative edging is less important than root-zone health.
