# Chapter 7 - Soil Care, Mulch and Fertilization

## 7.1 Soil is the long-term system

The objective is not to create a perfect planting hole. It is to develop a broad, biologically active, uncompacted root zone. The Mongolia nursery report identifies good soil preparation as a foundation of quality seedling production and discusses cultivation, organic fertilizers and irrigation infrastructure.

## 7.2 Compaction control

Do not park vehicles over root zones, store construction materials beneath trees, repeatedly walk on wet soil, or cultivate deeply through tree roots. Use defined paths and broad mulch beds.

## 7.3 Mulch materials

Suitable materials may include composted wood chips, shredded bark, well-composted plant material and clean local organic material free from weed seed.

Avoid fresh manure against roots, contaminated sawdust, plastic sheet over the root zone, thick layers touching trunks and salty ash.

## 7.4 Mulch installation

- remove aggressive perennial weeds;
- water dry soil first;
- spread mulch broadly;
- maintain approximately 5-8 cm depth;
- taper to zero near the trunk;
- refresh when decomposed;
- inspect for rodent shelter.

A larger mulch area is generally more useful than a deep small ring.

## 7.5 Compost

Use mature compost with an earthy smell and stable texture. Apply it as a thin topdressing beneath mulch, incorporate it across an entire new shrub bed, or use it gradually to improve sandy or disturbed soils.

Avoid filling the planting hole with a high percentage of compost. Roots must transition into the surrounding soil.

## 7.6 Manure

Only well-composted manure should be considered. Fresh manure can burn roots, introduce weeds and add excessive salts or nitrogen. Keep manure away from the trunk and do not use it as the direct planting medium.

## 7.7 Fertilizer decision process

Fertilize only when a soil test identifies a deficiency, plant symptoms and site evidence support a nutrient problem, nursery production requires a controlled program, or growth remains poor after water, drainage, root depth and compaction are corrected.

Water problems are more common than fertilizer deficiency in newly planted trees.

## 7.8 Timing

- avoid strong fertilizer at planting;
- if needed, apply measured nutrients during active growth;
- avoid late-season high-nitrogen applications that promote soft growth before winter;
- water appropriately after granular applications.

The STREAM nursery report recommends regular fertilization during vegetative growth for nursery production. Nursery production is intensive; do not transfer nursery rates directly to a home landscape.

## 7.9 Reading fertilizer labels

A label such as 10-10-10 indicates percentages by weight of nitrogen, phosphate equivalent and potash equivalent. Follow the product label and soil-test recommendation.

## 7.10 Micronutrients

Iron deficiency-like yellowing can occur in alkaline soil, but yellow leaves can also result from wet roots, root damage or cold soil. Do not apply micronutrients until the cause is reasonably established.

## 7.11 Mycorrhiza products

Commercial inoculants may be useful in specific sterile or severely disturbed systems, but they do not replace appropriate species, living roots, correct planting depth, water, oxygen and organic matter.

## 7.12 Weed management

Preferred sequence:

1. mulch;
2. hand removal while weeds are small;
3. mowing outside the mulch zone;
4. targeted professional control where necessary.

The Mongolia nursery report recommends early and repeated weeding, mowing outside beds and control before weeds set seed. Home users should not copy nursery chemical practices without confirming current legal products and label directions.

## 7.13 Lawn and tree conflict

Turf competes for water and encourages mower damage. Maintain a grass-free mulched area around young trees. Do not use a string trimmer close to bark.

## 7.14 Soil-care checklist

Each spring and autumn:

- check mulch depth;
- expose buried root flares;
- remove trunk-contacting material;
- inspect drainage;
- expand mulch as the crown grows;
- test soil only when needed;
- correct compaction through professional methods rather than deep digging through roots.
