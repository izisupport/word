# Chapter 11 - Propagation and a Small Home Nursery

## 11.1 Decide whether to propagate

Propagation is useful for caragana hedges, sea buckthorn, willow, poplar, selected native or locally adapted trees, replacement plants and community planting. Do not collect seed or cuttings where collection is prohibited. Maintain genetic diversity; do not propagate an entire shelterbelt from one weak clone.

## 11.2 Nursery site

Select a site with reliable clean water, good access, drainage, protection from livestock, wind protection, sunlight and adjustable shade, room for beds and paths, and clean storage.

The Mongolia nursery report emphasizes infrastructure, soil preparation, irrigation, shading, weed control and production planning.

## 11.3 Seed source

Record species, collection location, elevation, parent trees, date, collector, treatment and germination result. Use healthy, well-formed local parent trees where permitted. Respect provenance.

## 11.4 Seed cleaning and storage

- remove debris and damaged seed;
- dry only to the level appropriate for the species;
- label every lot;
- store cool, dry or moist according to seed physiology;
- protect from rodents;
- test germination before large sowing.

Do not assume all seeds can be dried and frozen.

## 11.5 Dormancy treatments

### Cold-moist stratification

Used for many temperate woody species. Seed is mixed with a clean moist medium and held at low, above-freezing temperature for a species-specific period.

### Scarification

Hard seed coats may be mechanically abraded or treated by a species-specific method. Incorrect treatment can kill seed. Use a verified species protocol.

## 11.6 Growing medium

A nursery medium must balance water retention, drainage, aeration, cleanliness, nutrient supply and structural stability. FAO's *Management of Forest Nurseries* covers topsoil, sand, compost and soil mixing. Do not copy a universal ratio without testing local materials.

## 11.7 Sowing

- sow at the correct season;
- use uniform depth;
- avoid overcrowding;
- label rows;
- water gently;
- protect from wind, birds and rodents;
- use clean seedbeds.

The Mongolia nursery report warns that overcrowded seedlings are more susceptible to thinning, fungal disease and drying.

## 11.8 Shading

The report states that Siberian larch and Scots pine are light-demanding and can handle direct sunlight, while Siberian pine and fir need protection from direct sun during nursery production. Regulated shading reduces evaporation and irrigation needs.

Use adjustable shade cloth, maintain airflow, raise shade above foliage, reduce shade gradually during hardening and avoid permanent deep shade for light-demanding species.

## 11.9 Watering nursery stock

Use a fine, even application that does not displace seed. Water according to medium moisture, seedling stage and weather. Avoid repeated shallow wetting, continuous saturation, drying of small containers and irrigation that leaves foliage wet overnight during cold periods.

## 11.10 Pricking out and transplanting

Move seedlings when they can be handled without severe root damage. Water before lifting, shade roots, hold by leaves or root collar rather than fragile stem, spread roots, plant at correct depth and water immediately.

## 11.11 Root pruning

The Mongolia nursery report recommends undercutting two-year-old Scots pine and Siberian larch seedlings from mid-July to mid-August in the described nursery system. It specifies an 8-10 cm depth, retaining roughly two-thirds of the root system, followed by sufficient irrigation. This stimulates fine-root development and improves planting quality.

This is a production technique requiring suitable tools, timing and post-treatment irrigation. Home growers should test it on a small batch.

## 11.12 Hardening

Before outplanting, reduce excessive nitrogen, increase exposure gradually, avoid continuous high moisture, allow tissues to mature, maintain root health and protect from severe frost. Hardening is not deliberate wilting.

## 11.13 Ornamental sapling production

The Mongolia report recommends open-ground cultivation for no more than about five years in the described system, transplanting at least once and preferably twice, promotion of a compact fibrous root system, regular watering, and avoiding prolonged confinement in plastic containers.

## 11.14 Lifting and transport

Plan planting before lifting, lift in cool conditions, protect roots immediately, bundle carefully, keep dark, cool and moist, minimize storage, transport promptly and plant immediately after arrival.

The report describes temporary storage above freezing and up to about 4°C with high humidity for nursery operations. Home growers should avoid storage whenever possible.

## 11.15 Nursery records

Track seed lot, sowing date, germination, bed or container, treatments, irrigation, fertilizer, disease, inventory, lifting, destination and survival after planting.
