# Chapter 5 - Correct Planting Procedure

## 5.1 Tools

Typical tools include a spade, shovel, pruning shears, knife for container removal, tarp, water hose or buckets, measuring stick, mulch, stakes and flexible ties where needed, protective guard and gloves.

## 5.2 Locate the root flare

The root flare is where the trunk broadens and major roots begin. It must remain at or slightly above the finished soil surface. Nursery soil may cover the flare. Remove excess medium from the top of the root ball until the first structural roots are visible.

Planting too deep is a major long-term cause of decline.

## 5.3 Dig a broad, shallow hole

The hole should be no deeper than the measured root system or root ball, substantially wider than the root ball, widest near the surface, and roughened at the sides if soil is smeared.

Place the tree on firm, undisturbed soil so it does not settle. In heavy or slowly drained soil, plant slightly high and grade soil gradually away from the root ball. Do not create a deep amended basin that traps water.

## 5.4 Bare-root planting

1. Keep roots covered until the moment of planting.
2. Trim only broken or dead root ends with clean tools.
3. Make the hole wide enough for roots to spread naturally.
4. Form a low mound if needed to support the root collar at grade.
5. Spread roots radially.
6. Backfill gradually with excavated soil.
7. Work soil gently among roots to remove large air pockets.
8. Do not bend roots upward or coil them in the hole.
9. Water thoroughly after backfilling.
10. Add mulch.

The STREAM silviculture handbook illustrates pit, slit and auger planting for forest seedlings. In a home landscape, broad-hole planting is generally preferred for ornamental stock because roots can be inspected and spread.

## 5.5 Container planting

1. Water the container before planting if the root ball is dry.
2. Remove the container completely.
3. Find the root flare.
4. Inspect the root ball.
5. Loosen fine matted roots.
6. Correct circling roots by teasing, slicing or removing defective roots as appropriate.
7. Place the tree on firm soil at correct depth.
8. Backfill with site soil.
9. Water slowly and deeply.
10. Mulch broadly.

Never plant a tree inside an intact plastic pot. Remove synthetic wrapping and ties.

## 5.6 Balled-and-burlapped planting

1. Position the root ball using the root flare, not the top of the wrapping.
2. Stabilize the ball.
3. Remove all synthetic rope, wire and non-degradable material.
4. Remove or fold natural burlap away from the upper root ball.
5. Backfill and water.

Use trained help for large stock.

## 5.7 Backfill soil

Use the excavated native soil as the principal backfill. Large differences between the planting pit and surrounding soil can discourage roots from extending outward.

Compost may be used conservatively across a broad planting bed when soil organic matter is low, but do not create a rich isolated pocket. Do not place fresh manure or concentrated fertilizer against roots.

## 5.8 Watering basin

On level or gently sloping ground, form a low temporary berm outside the root ball to retain irrigation water. Do not pile soil against the trunk. On heavy, poorly drained soil, avoid a basin that keeps the root collar continuously wet.

## 5.9 Mulch

Apply an organic mulch layer approximately 5-8 cm deep over a broad area, adjusted for material texture. Keep mulch several centimeters away from the trunk.

Benefits include reduced evaporation, moderated soil temperature, reduced weed competition, improved soil structure over time and protection from mower injury. Avoid mulch volcanoes.

## 5.10 Staking

Stake only when the root ball is unstable or the site is severely windy.

- use two low stakes outside the root ball;
- use wide flexible ties;
- allow slight trunk movement;
- prevent rubbing;
- inspect monthly;
- remove ties after establishment, commonly within one growing season.

## 5.11 Protection

Use guards where there is risk from livestock, dogs, rodents, browsing wildlife, vehicles, string trimmers or snow shoveling. Guards must not trap moisture against the bark or constrict the trunk.

## 5.12 First watering

Water immediately after planting, even if soil appears moist. Apply water slowly. Stop if water remains ponded because the soil cannot absorb it.

## 5.13 Planting inspection

After watering, confirm that the root flare remains visible, the tree is vertical, soil has not settled below the root ball, mulch does not touch the trunk, ties are not tight, water infiltrates and branches are not damaged.

## 5.14 What not to do

- do not bury the trunk;
- do not add fertilizer to the planting hole;
- do not prune healthy crown growth to balance root loss;
- do not leave roots exposed to wind;
- do not place gravel at the bottom as a universal drainage solution;
- do not wrap wire tightly around the trunk;
- do not water every day without checking soil moisture.
